# Theorems for Free!

## Reference

Philip Wadler, “Theorems for Free!,” in *FPCA '89: Proceedings of the Fourth
International Conference on Functional Programming Languages and Computer
Architecture* (ACM, 1989), 347–359.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/99370.99404) and
[official bibliographic record](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/theorems-for-free).

## Contribution

Wadler turns Reynolds's abstraction theorem into a programmer-facing
calculus: derive an equation about a polymorphic function from its type rather
than from its implementation.

## Method

The paper translates types into relational propositions and specializes the
resulting parametricity statements to graphs of functions. Worked examples
derive concrete equations for polymorphic list and higher-order functions.

## Findings

- Every parametric inhabitant of a type satisfies the relational statement
  generated by that type.
- For familiar types, specializing the relation to a function graph produces
  equations resembling naturality. A polymorphic transformation cannot
  manufacture, inspect, or rearrange element values except as its type permits.
- These equations can support program transformation and representation
  independence without reading the term's implementation.
- The theorem constrains values, not costs. Two extensionally equal sides may
  differ in termination or performance once the language admits bottom and
  strictness observations; the paper discusses such qualifications for a lazy
  setting.

## Relevance

This work makes the connection between polymorphism and categorical
naturality operational for API review. A Catena signature such as
`forall a. F a -> G a` should earn a natural-transformation reading only in a
fragment whose observations preserve parametricity. It also motivates a tool
that reports candidate laws from public polymorphic types, clearly labeled by
their assumptions.

## Limits

A free theorem is only as strong as the abstraction theorem underneath it.
General recursion, strict primitives, effects, runtime type information, and
unsafe operations can require admissibility or other side conditions. The
equations are not automatically safe optimization rules, and they say nothing
about whether the API is understandable.
