# Studying the Language and Structure in Non-Programmers' Solutions to Programming Problems

## Reference

John F. Pane, Chotirat Ann Ratanamahatana, Brad A. Myers. Studying the Language and Structure in Non-Programmers' Solutions to Programming Problems. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 54(2), 237–264, 2001. [Canonical source](https://john.pane.net/IJHCS.html); [text consulted](https://john.pane.net/pdf/PaneRatanamahatanaMyers2001.pdf).

Reading locations: Study methods, discussion of operations on multiple objects, and conclusion.

## Method

Two elicitation studies inspect how non-programmers describe solutions, first around games and animation and then another audience and data-processing domain.

## Findings

Participants often described aggregate operations and sets rather than explicit iteration machinery. Their answers also contained ambiguity and underspecification. Natural problem expression therefore does not imply adopting unrestricted natural-language syntax.

## Limits

The studies identify tendencies and design possibilities, not a validated grammar for expert functional programming. Their populations and task domains differ from Catena's.

## Relevance

Use domain tasks and collection transformations as the entry path. Preserve precise scope, boundary conditions, and evaluation rules even when the explanation sounds ordinary.
