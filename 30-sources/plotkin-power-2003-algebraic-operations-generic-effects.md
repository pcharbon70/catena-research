# Algebraic Operations and Generic Effects

## Reference

Gordon Plotkin and John Power, “Algebraic Operations and Generic Effects,”
*Applied Categorical Structures* 11, no. 1 (2003): 69–94.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023064908962),
[author manuscript](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/gdp/publications/alg_ops_gen_effects.pdf),
and [official bibliographic record](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/algebraic-operations-and-generic-effects/).

## Research question

What coherence condition distinguishes operations that are algebraic for a
computational monad, and how do operation-style constructs correspond to
generic effects that directly request a result?

## Method

The paper works in an enriched categorical setting with a strong monad. It
gives several equivalent formulations of algebraic operations, relates them
through enriched naturality and monad multiplication, and uses an enriched
Yoneda argument to establish a bijection between algebraic operations and
generic effects. It then instantiates the account with examples and
nonexamples from computational effects.

## Findings

- Algebraic operations are natural in the Kleisli category. Operationally,
  this is the continuation-compatibility property that lets later sequencing
  distribute uniformly into an operation's continuation positions.
- Under the paper's assumptions, giving an algebraic operation is equivalent
  to giving its generic effect. Binary nondeterministic choice and a request
  for an arbitrary Boolean illustrate the two presentations.
- The framework covers operations associated with nondeterminism, exceptions,
  I/O, and state. It permits infinitary arities where required.
- An exception-raising operation can be algebraic while exception handling is
  a deconstructor and is not algebraic in the same sense.
- Continuation operations are identified as a notable nonexample because they
  depend directly on the continuation rather than commuting uniformly with
  it.
- Effects may be generated by operations subject to equations, connecting the
  account to algebraic theories rather than treating “algebraic” as a synonym
  for any side effect.

## Relevance

This paper supplies the technical meaning of *algebraic* used by the synthesis.
It prevents two design errors: calling every control construct algebraic, and
assuming handler syntax alone explains why operations compose with later
computation. It also motivates the separation between an operation that builds
a request and a handler that deconstructs or interprets it.

## Limits

The paper is a denotational foundation, not a language design. It does not
specify effect rows, handler lookup, deep versus shallow resumptions, runtime
representation, diagnostics, or resource cleanup. Its equivalence theorems
depend on categorical assumptions that a Catena calculus would have to
instantiate rather than cite by analogy.
