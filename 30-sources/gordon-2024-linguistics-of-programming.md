# The Linguistics of Programming

## Reference

Colin S. Gordon. The Linguistics of Programming. Onward! 2024, 162–182, 2024. [Canonical source](https://csgordon.github.io/publications/onward24/); [text consulted](https://csgordon.github.io/publications/onward24/onward24.pdf).

Reading locations: Section 3.1 on pragmatics and Section 3.2 on language acquisition, particularly pp. 168–170.

## Method

An analytical essay connects linguistics with programming-language and software-engineering questions. It reinterprets earlier studies through transfer, pragmatics, and language acquisition.

## Findings

Prior language knowledge can shape expectations about new notation. Task wording can invite pragmatic interpretations beyond literal meaning. These are explanatory proposals, not newly measured Catena effects.

## Limits

The essay is not a controlled comparison of syntax designs. Transfer from natural-language research remains an argument to test in programming contexts.

## Relevance

Include misleading familiarity in Catena's review: ordinary words such as context, pure, and parallel can suggest guarantees absent from a contract. Pilot task wording as carefully as candidate code.
